How the match gets won: take the bottom courts decisively and make the top two competitive. This is the match where a clean sweep is genuinely available, and where the courts we bank could decide our placing.

How They See Us

They will pull our league sheet and find 25–7 in courts, the second-best record among the five, and a roster whose full body of work — .619 across 370 courts — is the strongest number in the draw. On paper we are nobody's easy match.

Then they'll pull last August. Nine of our eleven were here, played 33 courts, and went 15–18. Whoever scouts us sees a team that is strong at home and has already been measured at this level. Nobody in this draw is afraid of us. Nobody is dismissing us either. We're the team they pencil in for a close one.

One thing we do own outright: more Sectionals experience than anyone else in the field. Nine returning men and 33 courts of it — more men and more courts than OK, IA or NE bring. Nobody here will be surprised by the venue, the heat, or the format.

Why Every Game Matters
The order ties get broken in, straight from the championship regulations. Read the first line twice.
#Tiebreaker
1Individual matches won. Total courts across the whole weekend — not team matches. Every single court counts the same, in a blowout or a loss.
2Head-to-head, only if all tied teams played each other and one beat them all.
3Fewest sets lost.
4Fewest games lost.
5Game winning percentage.
6A method determined by the championships committee.

There is no dead rubber and no dead set

If you're down a set and a break to a better team, the games you claw back are worth something real — they're step four, and in a five-team round robin ties are the norm, not the exception.

Losing 4–6 5–7 and losing 0–6 0–6 are different results in the standings.

What This Analysis Cannot Tell You
  • Pair strength is a rating proxy, not a result. "Strongest pair" means highest combined current TR dynamic rating, applied backwards to matches played Sept 2025–July 2026. A player who has improved since makes an old lineup look better-stacked than the captain intended.
  • Samples are small and unequal. Six to ten team matches per opponent. STL's 6-of-6 at D3 is a real signal; NE's 0.13 gap on eight matches is one match from vanishing, and is reported as "flat" rather than an inversion for that reason.
  • Court-slot counts are scoped to each team's own schedule only, excluding the same players' appearances in 18+, 55+, Tri-Level and so on, because those cards were ordered by a different captain under a different court count. The per-player lines in the match cards are not scoped this way and will disagree — this page is the narrower and, for stacking, the correct one.
  • Two opponents played five-court leagues (IA, OK). In their record D3 is a middle court, not a bottom one. Their predicted cards are the least reliable in the field for the same reason.
  • Every lineup strategy sorts on rating or partnership count. None sorts on winning. That is why Jerry Martin benches at 5–0.
  • The builder cannot express two matches in one day, or a per-player match cap. Stated limits of the tool, not oversights.
  • Nothing has been re-pulled since Aug 14. All four league seasons had ended by then so the match record is effectively final — but dynamic ratings move weekly, and a late roster add would not appear.
  • No indoor/outdoor confirmation. Outdoor assumed. Late-August Springfield heat is a real factor in a two-match Saturday and is in none of these numbers — see the weather page.
