Match Rules

The tournament's on-court rules

On Court

  • Weather move: If outdoor matches are moved indoors due to weather, a $5 per person fee will apply.
  • Warm-up: 10 minute warm-up with opponent, including serves.
  • Score tenders: All players should use the score tenders on court at all times.
  • Line calls: Any ball that cannot be called out with certainty is good.
  • Finishing: At completion of match, tap rackets and confirm score before leaving court.

Tiebreakers

Standings Tiebreakers
Applied across the entire competition when teams finish level. Read the first line twice.
#Standings tiebreaker
1Individual matches. Winner of the most individual matches in the entire competition — courts, not team matches. Every court counts the same, in a blowout or in a loss.
2Head-to-head. Winner of the head-to-head match, if all tied teams play each other.
3Sets. Loser of the fewest number of sets.
4Games. Loser of the fewest number of games.
5Game win %. Total games won divided by total games played.
6Doubles 1. Winner of the Doubles 1 court.
Match Tiebreakers
Applied inside one team match. Four courts, so the individual-match count settles it at 4–0 or 3–1 and the rest of the list is for 2–2.
#Match tiebreaker
1Individual matches. 4–0 or 3–1.
2Sets. Loser of the fewest number of sets.
3Games. Loser of the fewest number of games.
4Game win %. Total games won divided by total games played.
5Doubles 1. Winner of the Doubles 1 court.

There is no dead rubber and no dead set

If you're down a set and a break to a better team, the games you claw back are worth something real — games are the fourth line of the standings list and the third line inside a match, and in a five-team round robin ties are the norm, not the exception.

Losing 4–6 5–7 and losing 0–6 0–6 are different results in the standings.

They will pull our league sheet and find 25–7 in courts, the second-best record among the five, and a roster whose full body of work — .619 across 370 courts — is the strongest number in the draw. On paper we are nobody's easy match.

Then they'll pull last August. Nine of our eleven were here, played 33 courts, and went 15–18. Whoever scouts us sees a team that is strong at home and has already been measured at this level. Nobody in this draw is afraid of us. Nobody is dismissing us either. We're the team they pencil in for a close one.

One thing we do own outright: More Sectionals experience than anyone else in the field. Nine returning men and 33 courts of it — more men and more courts than OK, IA or NE bring. Nobody here will be surprised by the venue, the heat, or the format.

Why Every Game Matters
Why the scoreline in a match already lost still moves the standings. The order itself is on the Tiebreakers page.

The first line of the standings list is individual matches won across the entire competition — courts, not team matches. Every court counts the same in a blowout as in a win, and the games you claw back out of a lost set are the fourth line. In a five-team round robin ties are the norm, not the exception, so losing 4–6 5–7 and losing 0–6 0–6 are different results in the standings.

Tiebreakers carries both orders in full — the standings ladder and the one applied inside a single team match.
